    Why this is the whole idea

    A permission is a flag, and a flag copies for free. Ten agents each holding can_search: true can search forever, and the eleventh agent they spawn can too. Nothing in that model can answer “how much”.

    LETS makes authority a quantity. An agent holds a lease on a slice of a finite envelope, and spawning a child is an atomic escrow out of that slice — the parent's residual falls by exactly what the child receives, in the same instant. That is why the bars above always move in opposite directions and never both grow. A tree of a thousand agents cannot hold more than the root was given, because there is no operation that adds.

    The second half is that a child's capabilities can only ever be a subset of its parent's. Attenuation is one-way. An agent cannot hand its child a power it was never given, so no amount of delegation depth can launder a capability into existence.

    Both refusals above are the runtime's own, not this page's invention. You can reproduce them against a live warden on the register, where the red text comes straight off the wire, and the conservation panel re-derives the arithmetic rather than trusting the warden's own health flag.
